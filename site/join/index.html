What you'll see on GitHub

Every way of taking part goes through the same 5 screens. Just press the button with the green glow at each step. On a phone, everything works the same in your browser.

Make your own copy. The first time, GitHub asks you to make a copy of the project (called a fork) so you can suggest a change. Press the green button. You only do this once.

What happens next

  1. Automatic checks look for duplicates and formatting problems, and leave a comment if anything needs fixing.
  2. A mentor reviews your contribution, usually within 48 hours.
  3. When it's added, you get an email with your badge, one-click Add to LinkedIn and Share on X buttons, and a public verification page.
  4. Your name appears on the leaderboard, and your contribution gets its own page that anyone can read.

Stuck? Quick answers

GitHub asks me to sign in.Sign in, or create a free account, then press the "Open GitHub" button on this page again. I don't see "Fork this repository".That's fine: you already have a copy from before. GitHub goes straight to the editor. I can't find "Commit changes…".It's at the top right of the editor. On a small phone screen, scroll up or turn your phone sideways. I made a mistake after creating the pull request.No problem. Leave a comment on your pull request saying what's wrong and a mentor will help you fix it. The checks left a comment saying something needs fixing.Read the comment, it says exactly what to change. Then open your pull request, go to Files changed, tap ⋯ → Edit file, fix it and commit again. The checks run again on their own. Do I need to know how to code?No. Tips, posts, quizzes, stories and checklists only need plain writing. What can't I share?Anything harmful: malware, phishing kits, real passwords, personal data or attacks on real organisations. Everything here is for education and defence only. Still stuck?Ask in Discussions or email dearlolami@gmail.com. No question is too basic.
